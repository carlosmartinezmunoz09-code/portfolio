Who I am

I'm heading into aerospace engineering. The part of it that keeps pulling me back is high-speed flight — specifically the fluid dynamics problems.

I'm based in Dubai and have spent the last couple of years building technical experience through a mix of my own projects, a school racing team, and The Knowledge Society programme. The thread connecting most of it is a preference for doing things — making physical objects, running simulations, building and breaking prototypes — over treating engineering as a purely theoretical exercise.

3D Printing & Fabrication

3D printing is the fastest way I have to close the loop between an idea and something I can physically test. I use it for airframe iterations on the EDF rocket, structural mock-ups for aerodynamic components, and for fabricating the riblet geometry test samples from the APEX biomimetic skin work. The print speed matters — being able to run through several geometry versions in a week changes how I design.

The main lesson from doing this repeatedly is that the design constraints you discover once a part is in your hands are almost never the ones you anticipated on screen. Surface finish, layer adhesion under load, and the places where a printed part flexes differently from how a solid model predicts — these only show up when you build the thing.

Writing the space printing article extended this interest into a different context: what would fabrication need to look like to work in orbit, with no gravity, no convection cooling, and feedstocks that bear no resemblance to polymer filament?

The Knowledge Society — TKS

TKS put me in situations where I had to understand a company well enough to argue a non-obvious position about it in public, and then defend that position under questioning. The XPANCEO pitch — framing smart contact lenses as a space medicine tool for SANS detection rather than a consumer wearable — placed 3rd in the cohort. The Lovable pitch, building a secondary education growth strategy, made UAE finals.

What changed most from TKS wasn't the technical knowledge — it was the discipline of getting to the actual interesting question fast. Most pitches fail because they spend too long on what the product does and not enough on why the framing they've chosen is the right one. Learning to lead with the argument changed how I present everything, including technical work.
